    Stuck? The 4-stroke cycle in plain English

    A stroke is one trip of the piston, either down or up. A 4-stroke engine needs 4 trips (2 full turns of the crankshaft) to make 1 power push.

    1. Intake (Suck): piston goes down, intake valve open, exhaust closed. The falling piston makes a vacuum that pulls in fuel and air from the carburetor.
    2. Compression (Squeeze): piston goes up, both valves closed. The mix is squeezed small and gets hot. Right at the end, the spark plug fires.
    3. Power (Bang): the burning mix expands and shoves the piston down. Both valves are still closed. This is the only stroke that makes power. The heavy flywheel keeps the engine spinning through the other three.
    4. Exhaust (Blow): piston goes up, exhaust valve open. The burned gas is pushed out to the muffler.

    Why the cam turns at half speed: each valve opens once every 2 crank turns. The cam gear has twice as many teeth as the crank gear, so it turns once per cycle.

    Wasted spark: the Magnetron fires every time the flywheel magnet passes the coil, which is once per turn. One spark is at the end of compression (useful). The other is at the end of exhaust, when there is nothing to burn.
