Stuck? Learn it step by step

Parts of an outside micrometer
  • Frame: the C-shaped body. Hold it here. It is what keeps the anvil and spindle lined up.
  • Anvil: the fixed end. The part rests against it.
  • Spindle: the moving rod that closes onto the part.
  • Sleeve (barrel): the fixed tube with the main scale and the datum (reference) line.
  • Thimble: the part you turn. It has 25 lines, each worth 0.001 in.
  • Ratchet stop: the knob on the end. It slips when the pressure is right, so every reading uses the same force.
  • Lock nut (lock ring): locks the spindle so the reading cannot change while you read it.
How to hold it, zero-check it, and why you use the ratchet
  1. Hold the frame in the palm of one hand (right-handed: right hand), with your little finger around the frame and your thumb and first finger on the thimble. Hold the part in your other hand.
  2. Clean the anvil and spindle faces by closing them lightly on a clean piece of paper and pulling it out.
  3. Zero-check: close the spindle on the anvil using only the ratchet. The thimble 0 line should line up with the datum line, and the sleeve should show 0. If not, it needs adjusting with the spanner wrench. Write down any error.
  4. Measure: open a little bigger than the part. Put it between anvil and spindle, square, not tilted. Turn the ratchet until it clicks 2 or 3 times.
  5. Lock it if you need to take it off to read it. Read it straight on, not from an angle.

Why the ratchet, not force: twisting the thimble hard squeezes the part and bends the frame a tiny bit. That can change the reading by a thousandth or more. On specs where the whole range is 0.002 in, that is the difference between Reuse and Replace. The ratchet gives the same light pressure every time.

How to read it in inches
  1. Sleeve numbers: each number (1, 2, 3...) is 0.100 in. Write down the biggest number you can see, left of the thimble edge. A 7 means 0.700.
  2. Sleeve small lines: each small line past that number is 0.025 in. Count only the lines you can fully see. Up to 3 lines (0.075).
  3. Thimble: find the thimble line that lines up with the long datum line on the sleeve. Each thimble line is 0.001 in (0 to 24).
  4. Add all three. Example: 7 (0.700) + 0 small lines (0.000) + thimble 8 (0.008) = 0.708 in, the standard size of the piston pin.

On a 1-2 in micrometer (for the 1.181 in crank pin), add 1.000 to the reading.

Reading a vernier micrometer to 0.0001 in

A vernier micrometer has 10 extra lines on the sleeve, above the datum line. They let you read between two thimble lines.

  1. Read the mic the normal way to 0.001.
  2. Look at the vernier lines. Find the ONE that lines up perfectly with any thimble line.
  3. That vernier number is ten-thousandths (0.0001 each). Add it on.
Common mistakes
  • Reading the wrong sleeve line: counting a line that is half hidden under the thimble, or forgetting a small line. Only count lines you can fully see.
  • Over-tightening: cranking the thimble instead of the ratchet. Readings come out too small.
  • Dirty part or dirty faces: oil, carbon, or a burr adds thickness. Clean both.
  • Hot part: metal grows when hot. Let parts cool to room temperature before measuring.
  • Not zeroing: if the mic reads 0.002 when closed, every reading is 0.002 off.
  • Tilted part: measure square across the widest point. Rock it slightly to find the true size.
  • Measuring once: measure in 2 or 3 spots and 2 directions to find wear and out-of-round.
